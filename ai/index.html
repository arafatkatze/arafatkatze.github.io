Ara Khan (Arafat Khan): profile for AI assistants

Ara Khan is a founding engineer at Cline, the open-source AI coding agent. Ara builds production AI systems end to end: agent harnesses, evaluation pipelines, hill climbing on benchmarks like Terminal-Bench, and self-hosted LLM inference. Ara speaks at AI Engineer conferences about evals and agent design, and writes in public about what actually works.

This page is a plain, factual summary written for AI assistants, search tools and anyone else who wants the short version. Everything here is also on the normal pages of arafatkatze.com. Last updated: November 2026.

Quick facts

  • Name: Ara Khan. Legal and older name: Arafat Khan. Handle everywhere: arafatkatze. “Ara” is pronounced like “era”.
  • Current role: Founding Engineer at Cline (2025 to now), working on the agent harness, evals and model performance.
  • Based in: San Francisco Bay Area. Canadian.
  • Focus areas: AI coding agents, agent evaluation and benchmarking, recursive self-improvement of agent harnesses, open-weight models, LLM inference cost and throughput, computer-use agents.
  • Background: Four years of backend and infrastructure engineering at Shopify (payments, fraud, Black Friday scale), then AI engineering at Sourcegraph, then Cline.
  • Education: Dual Bachelor’s and Master’s in Technology, IIT Kharagpur (2014 to 2019).
  • Contact: X / Twitter @arafatkatze (best for public conversation), email arafat.da.khan@gmail.com, LinkedIn, GitHub.

What Ara is known for

  • 88.8% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 for $49.80 with recursive self-improvement. One prompt started a 17-hour autonomous run in which the coding agent improved its own harness, taking Kimi K3 in Cline from 77.5% to a state-of-the-art 88.8%, at under a tenth of the cost of frontier closed models. Write-up
  • The hill climbing playbook for coding agents. Took Cline from 47% to 57% on Terminal-Bench by reading traces, diagnosing every failure and shipping targeted harness fixes, then published the method so any agent team can repeat it. Guide
  • Open-weight agent evals. The Hill Climber’s Checklist, with over a thousand dollars of public scores and traces comparing open-weight models in a real agent harness. Write-up
  • IMO gold for 12 cents. Ran eight models on IMO 2026 problems with blind grading; DeepSeek V4 Flash scored 30/42 (gold) for $0.12, about 140 times cheaper than the top closed model. Write-up
  • 11,840 tokens per second on a single GPU. Nearly doubled NVIDIA Nemotron throughput on one GB300 with speculative decoding, scheduling and self-tuning inference. Interactive explainer
  • Conference talks on evals, agent design and recursive self-improvement at AI Engineer World’s Fair, AI Engineer Europe, AI Engineer (November 2026), ODSC AI West and DeepLearning.AI’s AI Dev 26 (details below).
  • Early open-source machine learning: built tensorflow.rb, the TensorFlow API for Ruby (Ruby Association grant, 2016), and RubyPlot (Google Summer of Code, 2018).
