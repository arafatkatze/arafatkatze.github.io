Last year, I lost my job. I expected the layoff to feel catastrophic. Instead, I felt relief. I had a nice apartment, a rental agreement, and all the usual signs of stability, but I wanted permission to let them go.

I got rid of almost everything I owned and compressed my life into an 18-kilo suitcase. Then I left to see how little I needed. Coffee with a stranger who became a friend and a sunset on a foreign beach stayed with me longer than anything I had owned.

Part of the reason I travel so much is that I have been looking for a place I could call home. I carried a checklist from country to country, waiting for one place to satisfy it. None did. I began building a sense of home from the people around me and the routines I carried between cities.

This was privileged homelessness: I chose it, had money to travel, and could stop. It still changed my sense of what I need. My life fit into one suitcase, and the relationships survived every border.
